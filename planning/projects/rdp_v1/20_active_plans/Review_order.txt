Decisions I think you need to make

These are the real owner decisions still worth making now.

Decision 1 — how exact should the support matrix be in v1?

You need to decide whether each key cell is:

Yes

Where relevant

Limited

Not supported

The charter explicitly says the matrix must distinguish those meanings. 

rdp_v1_governance_charter_v3_ow…

This is probably the most important remaining owner decision.

Decision 2 — how narrow is the first-release SolverReport?

The docs are aligned that ScorerReport stays and a narrow stable SolverReport should exist, but the exact minimum still needs locking.

You do not need full final class design now.
You do need the minimum promise.

Decision 3 — what is the exact minimum retained state for formal rescoring/reconstruction?

Both the campaign spec and refactor plan are clear that reconstruction must be possible and that campaign summary should build from telemetry plus reports, not replace them. But the exact retained minimum still needs owner sign-off.

Decision 4 — how strong do you want the first-release campaign framework promise to be?

The campaign spec already says the framework should support:

full grids

targeted sweeps

stage narrowing

survivor carry-forward

retry

formal rescoring

reservoir-like batching patterns

What you may still want to decide is whether all of those are part of the public first-release promise, or whether some are “framework-supported but not guaranteed across all first-release campaign variants”.

Decision 5 — do you want individual ADR files now, or one ADR pack temporarily?

The handbook recommends lightweight ADRs as separate files with a standard shape.

This is a process decision, not an architecture decision, but it affects workflow discipline.

Decision 6 — do you want the private planning layout adopted exactly as the handbook suggests?

The handbook suggests a concrete folder layout under planning/working, planning/review, planning/decisions, planning/architecture, planning/agents, and planning/release.

You should decide whether to:

adopt it basically as written

or trim it slightly

My view: adopt it nearly as written.

Decision 7 — what counts as owner sign-off versus maintainer discretion?

The handbook already says support reduction is architectural and needs explicit written owner sign-off. 

maintainer_handbook_v2

It would help to decide this explicitly:

Owner sign-off required for:

support reduction

weakening LP/no-WLI capability

second-surface exceptions

narrowing campaign seriousness

output/privacy contract weakening

Maintainer discretion allowed for:

internal naming

exact package names

small bridge mechanics with exit notes

minor contract-preserving cleanups

Decision 8 — what is the review acceptance bar for “ready to implement”?

The refactor plan already gives the gates:

document freeze

support wording freeze

current-code mapping note

anti-drift guardrails 

rdp_v1_refactor_plan_v2_owner_r…

You should explicitly say whether all four are required before any implementation starts.
My recommendation: yes.

You do not need to decide now:

exact final campaign-summary analysis space

advanced long-term generator/search helper design

The campaign spec explicitly leaves those flexible for implementation planning or later evolution.

Require:

task card

related tests named

This is directly aligned with the handbook’s task-field and guardrail rules.

During work

Require:

small slices

TODO status updated

no hidden side contracts

no public-contract widening casually

parity notes kept honest 

maintainer_handbook_v2

Require:

intended behaviour preserved or explicitly approved as changed

support-matrix cells protected by tests

no new absolute-path leakage
